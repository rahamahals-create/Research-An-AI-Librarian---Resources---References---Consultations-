# AI librarian consultation guide

Use this guide to structure discovery conversations before building or buying an AI librarian assistant.

## 1. Stakeholder map

Identify representatives from:

- Reference and instruction staff.
- Access services and circulation.
- Systems, discovery, and web teams.
- Privacy, legal, procurement, or institutional compliance.
- Accessibility and user-experience specialists.
- Student, faculty, public, or community patron groups.
- Library leadership and funders.

## 2. Discovery questions

### Service goals

- Which patron questions consume the most staff time but are low risk?
- Which services should remain human-only?
- What languages, devices, and accessibility needs should be supported?
- What does a successful pilot look like after 30, 60, and 90 days?

### Content and retrieval

- Which sources are authoritative for each answer type?
- Which licensed resources can be described, searched, or linked but not indexed?
- Who approves source additions, removals, and refresh schedules?
- How will the assistant handle missing, conflicting, or stale information?

### Privacy and data governance

- What patron data is needed to answer the question?
- What data must never be sent to a model or vendor?
- How long are chats, logs, ratings, and analytics retained?
- Can patrons use the service anonymously or without account linking?

### Safety and escalation

- What topics require an immediate staff handoff?
- What disclaimers should appear for specialized research, legal, medical, financial, or crisis-adjacent topics?
- How can patrons report a bad answer?
- Who reviews incidents and decides whether to pause the service?

### Evaluation

- What benchmark questions represent real local patron needs?
- How will citation accuracy and source coverage be measured?
- What bias, accessibility, and multilingual tests are required?
- What thresholds must be met before public launch?

## 3. Consultation outputs

Each consultation should produce:

1. A prioritized use-case list.
2. A risk register with owners and mitigations.
3. An approved-source register.
4. A patron-data flow diagram.
5. Evaluation criteria and launch gates.
6. A decision log that records unresolved questions.

## 4. Pilot launch questions

Do not launch until the team can answer:

- Who owns day-to-day monitoring?
- Who can update prompts and sources?
- Who can disable the assistant in an incident?
- How will patrons know they are using AI?
- How will staff be trained to interpret and correct AI-assisted answers?
